# 01_Oncology_PartSM_Audit: A flagship forensic audit project

This is a simulated HTA case study designed to showcase the remediation of structural modeling flaws (time-unit mismatches, cost misallocations, and hazard discontinuities) commonly found in complex oncology submissions.

---

## 🔍 Project Overview
This project documents the end-to-end technical remediation of a legacy economic model for Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma (RRMM). It addresses the critical need for biological plausibility and statistical transparency in HTA submissions, moving beyond "black-box" parameterization by implementing a robust **R-to-Excel analytical pipeline**.

## 🛠 Key Audit Findings (Remediation Log)
The forensic audit identified several structural vulnerabilities in the legacy architecture that were systematically remediated:

*   **Time-unit Misspecification**: Identified a mismatch between annualised hazard parameters and monthly cycle units, which caused an artificial "survival collapse".
*   **Cost Misallocation Bug**: Corrected a logic error where Progressed Disease (PD) costs were incorrectly assigned to the 'Death' state, distorting the true economic burden of life-extension.
*   **Methodological Hazard Cliff**: Resolved a discontinuous 0.70 HR jump at Month 61 by implementing a smoother, chained transition logic.

## 🚀 Methodological Innovation: Recursive Hazard Chaining
To resolve the common issue of "Hazard Jumps" at trial-to-extrapolation cut-offs, I developed a **Recursive Hazard Chaining Algorithm** in R:

*   **Hazard Anchoring**: Captures the instantaneous hazard at the 60-month anchor point to serve as the baseline for long-term extrapolation.
*   **Waning Phase Logic**: Implemented a linear waning period (Months 61-120) where the Treatment Hazard Ratio (HR) gradually reverts to 1.0, ensuring **biological plausibility** by preventing "immortal time bias".
*   **Pipeline Architecture**: R handles complex survival fitting and logical consistency checks; Excel serves as the auditable financial engine for NICE-style submissions.
